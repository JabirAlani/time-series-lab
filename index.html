AR order p

φ: weight on past values. Near +1, values stay high or low for a long time; negative, the series flips direction.

MA order q

θ: how much of a past shock carries into today. Near 0, surprises die out quickly.

Season length m

The same ideas at lag m, the S in SARIMA. 0 switches the term off.

Integration d

d = 1 adds up the ARMA series once (the I in ARIMA), so differencing once gets it back.

Symbols

φ(L), θ(L): the AR and MA polynomials in the lag operator L (L·yt = yt−1). Φ(Lm), Θ(Lm): their seasonal versions at lag m. (1 − L)d: differencing d times.

c is a constant, not quite the mean: the long-run mean of a stationary process is c / (1 − φ₁ − … − φₚ)(1 − Φ₁).

How to score

Choosing a forecasting approach

Naive baselines (last value, mean, same period last season): always compute them; they are the benchmark to beat.

Exponential smoothing: fast, robust and readable when trend and season are clear. No stationarity step, but no external drivers either.

ARIMA family: when the autocorrelation structure matters or you have external drivers (the X). Needs stationarity, handled with d and D.

All series are simulated in your browser, or pasted by you under “Your own numbers”. ARIMA-family models are fitted by conditional least squares with stationarity and invertibility enforced; exponential smoothing follows the ETS framework (Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice).
